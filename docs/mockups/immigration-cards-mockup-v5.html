How Many People Does ICE Remove?

Removals and detention by fiscal year

ICE’s headline removals figure for each year, split by which agency first apprehended the person: ICE’s own officers, or Border Patrol (who handed the person to ICE to remove). The line is the average number of people in ICE detention on a given day. FY2025 is preliminary.

Data notes

ICE only: Border Patrol’s own actions, including Title 42 expulsions, are not counted. Every removal here was carried out by ICE; the colours show who first apprehended the person (ICE’s “arresting agency”): ICE officers in the interior, or Border Patrol at the border, who handed the person to ICE. The Border Patrol part is ICE’s count of its own removals, not CBP’s figures. From FY2007 the count includes returns (voluntary returns, voluntary departures and withdrawals) as well as removals under an order, in both colours; before that it is removals only (hatched). ICE reports the split FY2008–FY2024; earlier years and FY2025 have a total only (grey). The detention line is a different kind of number: an average of the people held each day, not a count of people over the year, so it is not part of the bar total. ICE published it from FY2018; earlier years are blank. FY2025 (removals and detention) comes from a DHS budget document and is preliminary. Counts lock around October 5, so late closures roll into the next year.

Criminal history of those removed

Data notes

Criminal history is as recorded in ICE systems at the time of the enforcement action. Convictions include immigration crimes, and “no known record” means nothing is recorded in ICE systems; it is not a finding about the person. People who have just crossed the border usually have no U.S. record, and nearly all FY2023–24 “no known record” removals were people Border Patrol handed to ICE. From FY2017 you can view removals by who first apprehended the person; the Border Patrol view is the year’s total minus ICE’s own arrests, so it is derived, not printed by ICE. Through FY2015 ICE reports only convicted versus not convicted, and for FY2013–16 it prints no split by agency. FY2025 comes from a DHS budget document, is preliminary, and gives one combined convicted-or-pending figure with no further split. Title 42 expulsions are not counted as removals. Years with no ICE figure are blank, not zero.

Source: U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement removal statistics and annual enforcement reports, with FY2025 from the DHS FY2027 ICE budget overview. One agency, one definition: ICE only, never combined with Border Patrol or DHS-wide counts.
