What changed from v6 (mock chrome, not part of the page)
  1. Countries / Regions toggle on “Where refugees came from”, in the card header beside the year menu (the live card’s region-toggle spot and style). Regions swaps the list for the five regions, ranked by arrivals, each with its total and its cap; the bar is the ceiling bar from the chart above in miniature (pale bar and black cap, ranges shaded to the maximum, arrivals filled in). The map colours each country by its region, a row or a country on the map selects that region, and the rest fades. Everything else (row layout, header, Data notes, table) is unchanged.
  2. Caps are only as good as the data. The cap is the State Department’s year-end regional ceiling (else the last determination). FY2020–21 (split by kind of refugee) and FY2026 (“primarily” a group) have no regional caps, so those years say so in a note and show “–”. The unallocated reserve appears as its own cap-only row when a year has one.
  3. Change column uses + / − (not ▲ / ▼), here and on the live lists. It lines up as a number and can’t be mistaken for the sort toggle’s ▾; “0” means no change, because a bare dash would read as a minus; nothing is shown where there is no comparison.

How Many People Come In and Out of the U.S.?

Every year, the U.S. sends some people out and lets others in. Here are two pieces of that picture: who ICE removes from the country, and which refugees are resettled here from abroad.

How Many People Does ICE Remove?

ICE counts how many people it removes each fiscal year. Some were picked up at the border and handed over to ICE; the rest were arrested by ICE’s own officers.

Data notes

Ceiling: the most refugees the President authorizes for the year, a maximum rather than a quota, so arrivals under it are not a shortfall. It is the figure in force at year end, so a mid-year revision (FY2021, FY2026) shows the revised number; the tooltip gives the first. Admitted: refugees resettled from abroad, by fiscal year of arrival (State Department); asylum seekers, parolees and people with Temporary Protected Status are not included. Regions follow the applicant’s nationality. FY2026 is the first year-end report and may be revised. A year belongs to the president in office on Sept 30, so FY2025’s ceiling was set by the previous administration.
